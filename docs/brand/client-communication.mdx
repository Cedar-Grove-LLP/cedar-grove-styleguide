# Client Communication

Unlike the rest of this section — which *proposes* conventions the firm can adjust — **this
page is an adopted standard.** It reproduces Cedar Grove's **Email Style Guide**, the firm's
real, in-use standard for client email, so the product's written voice and the firm's everyday
client communication stay one and the same. Where the other Brand &amp; Content pages say
"recommendation," this one says "this is how we write."

Email is Cedar Grove's primary way of communicating with clients, and the firm sets out to
differentiate itself with clearer, faster, more respectful email than other firms. The method
is **Smart Brevity** (VandeHei, Allen &amp; Schwartz): carry the most meaning in the fewest
words. Emails should be **easy to scan, easy to act on, and respectful of the reader's time.**

## Core principles

### Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

Start with the update, decision, or ask — not the background.

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| "You're good to move forward." — "We reviewed X and recommend Y." | "I wanted to share some thoughts and context around the legal background of this issue…" — "I hope you're doing well and wanted to check in regarding the conversation we had the other week…" |

### One email = one message

Each email focuses on one clear thing — one **update**, one **decision**, or one **request**.
Don't mix updates, questions, and new topics; don't tack on "one more thing"; don't start new
threads inside replies. Multiple topics → clear sections or separate emails.

### Write for skimming

Assume the reader reads the subject, scans headers and first sentences, reads bullets, and
skips long paragraphs. Write for exactly that reader. Always ask: *what words can I remove?*
(The answer is almost always: some.)

## Greetings — the personal touch is a strength

Clarity is not coldness. A line that shows you remember something about the client's life, or a
congratulations on a recent win, goes a long way.

| Greet | Don't greet |
| --- | --- |
| **First email in a thread** — a short, warm, one-line greeting ("Hi Colin, hope you enjoyed Park City."). One line max. | **Replying in an ongoing thread** — skip the greeting and go straight to the point. It saves time and keeps the thread clean. |

## Recommended structure

Avoid the wall of text. Structure with short paragraphs and bullets.

1. **Subject** — Title Case, and include the (Client Name).
2. **Greeting** — one line max.
3. **Key answer / update / status / decision** — what's the answer, what changed, what's done, what's blocked.
4. **Context** — why the email exists; the detail behind the update.
5. **Action** (if any) — what the reader needs to do.
6. **Polite close** — short and professional.

## Formatting

- Standard **sans-serif** at normal size. **Black** for body text; **bold olive (`#5a5d4a`)**
  for inline replies within a thread.
- **Bold + underline** for section headers.
- Avoid **bold or en-dashes in the middle of running text** — it reads as AI-generated.

## Punctuation, grammar & word choice

- End every sentence with punctuation. Prefer short, clear sentences; avoid run-ons.
- Prefer the short, plain word: *explain* over *elucidate*, *everywhere* over *ubiquitous*,
  *bad* over *disconcerting*.
- Avoid abbreviations and overly casual shorthand, even internally (*lmk, hmu, wrt, ty, yep*).
- Emojis are acceptable but sparing — e.g. a smiley to close a light moment with someone you
  know well.

## Bullet points (strongly encouraged)

Use them to summarize updates, list completed tasks, or outline next steps.

- Start bullets with a verb where you can.
- Keep them short.
- Be consistent with punctuation — end every bullet with a period, or none at all.

## Tone

Professional, calm, and respectful. Direct but polite. Don't over-apologize.

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| "Totally understand if there hasn't been time yet — no worries at all." | "Sorry to bother you again, apologies in advance…" |

## Closings

Prefer **Best,** or **Thanks,**. No long or repetitive sign-offs.

## Before you send — the self-check

- Is the point clear in the first two lines?
- Can it be skimmed in 30 seconds?
- Did I use bullets instead of paragraphs?
- Is the tone polite and professional?
- Can I remove any extra words?

If yes to all → send.

## How this connects to the product

This standard is the source of truth for the firm's voice. The rest of **Brand &amp; Content**
carries the same instincts onto product surfaces — buttons, empty states, errors — where they
apply just as well: lead with the point, use the plainest word, don't over-apologize. Two
scoping notes where the surfaces differ:

- **Capitalization.** Email **subject lines** use Title Case (above). Product and marketing UI
  use **sentence case** — see **Grammar &amp; Mechanics**. Different surfaces, different rules.
- **Color.** The **`#5a5d4a`** inline-reply olive is the same brand olive the product uses as
  `primary` / `cedar-700` — a third independent sample of the logo ink. See
  **Foundations → Colors**.
