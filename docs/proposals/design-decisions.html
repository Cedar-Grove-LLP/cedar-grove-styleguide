Decision 1

The brand color

Your color isn’t invented — it’s sampled straight from your logo: an ivory ground (#ecede7) with a muted olive/moss ink (#585947). It’s a quiet, desaturated color, not a bright “cedar-tree” green. The color itself is settled — your Email Style Guide calls out the same olive (#5a5d4a) for emphasis, a third match to the logo. So the only open question is how far that olive spreads across the product.

Heritage

Recommended Live today

The olive is the primary action color everywhere — buttons, links, and focus rings all use it, across the client portal and internal tools alike.

Why we recommend it: it’s the highest-fidelity option — anyone who knows your mark recognizes it in every button they click — and there’s no accessibility cost. White on this olive lands at 6.73:1, comfortably above the readability floor.

The honest trade-off: a quiet olive repeated across a dense, data-heavy dashboard can start to read as flat at scale.

A text link

Ink-first

Olive as accent

Primary actions use a near-black warm charcoal instead. Olive is reserved strictly for accents — headers, tags, chart categories, marketing moments — so it only appears where it’s meant to draw the eye.

The case for it: in a dense internal tool, reserving the olive sharpens hierarchy and — counter-intuitively — makes the brand feel more distinctive by not becoming wallpaper. Contrast is even stronger: white on charcoal is 15.4:1.

The honest trade-off: less olive on screen overall; the brand color steps back from the everyday interface.

Accent tag

Evolved

Brightened olive

Keep olive as the primary, but nudge it slightly richer — the same hue, a touch more alive on a backlit screen than on a printed seal. A values-and-chroma tweak, not a color change.

The case for it: a middle ground — it keeps the heritage feel of Heritage while asking whether the exact letterhead value is the right one to scale up.

The honest trade-off: not built yet — it would need its own contrast pass and a call on “more alive” vs. “no longer the same color.”

Decision 2

The display typeface

Your body and interface text is Inter — that’s settled (it matches your live site, your Email Style Guide specifies a sans-serif too, and it’s built for screens). The open call is the display face for headings and titles that pairs with it. Three real candidates, each shown below in its actual typeface.

Fraunces

Recommended Live today

Cedar Grove LLP

We help good people build good organizations.

Why we recommend it: a warm, editorial serif with genuine character. Of the three it creates the clearest separation from the Inter body text — it reads as a deliberate typographic decision, and it’s the option most likely to feel at home next to your distinctive classical wordmark.

The honest trade-off: it’s the most opinionated of the three — a specific choice, not a neutral one. It rewards a little buy-in.

Almarai

On your site today

Cedar Grove LLP

We help good people build good organizations.

The case for it: a rounded, friendly geometric sans — and it’s the exact heading font your site uses right now (read from its own code). Choosing it means the marketing site and the product share one heading face with zero translation.

The honest trade-off: it’s a sans, so it doesn’t echo your serif wordmark, and it can read a touch more casual than the gravitas most firms want in large display type.

Source Serif 4

The safe middle
